Nathaniel School of Music/Course

learnmusic.nathanielschool.com

How a working producer actually runs a session

Not a REAPER course. The internet has one of those, it is free, and Kenny Gioia has owned it for a decade. This is about speed, organisation and delivery — taught through my own released projects, with these tools as the vehicle.

Not open yet Eight modules · four ship first learnmusic.nathanielschool.com

Who it is for, plainly

Somebody who already uses a DAW and is slow in it. Who can do everything in the list below, just not quickly, and not the same way twice.

It is not for beginners looking for a feature tour. If you have never opened REAPER, the free material out there is better than anything I would charge you for, and you should go and use it first.

The REAPER knowledge here is the medium, not the product. The product is a way of working: the same session structure every time, colour and naming that mean something, folders that hold, sections you can navigate blind, and a handoff the person downstream does not send back.

The eight modules

Each module has an artefact the student installs and uses, so nothing is watched passively. The mapping between modules and apps is deliberate.

  • 1 · The speed layerThe twelve keys. Why non-traditional bindings, what each one costs you, and how to design your own. Ends with the student's own keymap file. → The speed layer
  • 2 · Track organisation and colourColour as navigation rather than decoration. Rules, families, structure. → Palette & Look
  • 3 · Folders and signal flowHow REAPER folders actually work, why they break, and how to repair a session that has already gone wrong. → Folders & Flow
  • 4 · Sections and timeMarkers, regions and tempo maps that hold. → Sections & Time
  • 5 · Render and deliverStems a mixer will not send back. → Stem Print & Handoff
  • 6 · Recording at speedThe whole session-prep ritual end to end, on a real project, at real speed.
  • 7 · Collaboration and handoffTemplates, settings transfer, project portability. → Track Settings Transfer
  • 8 · LiveThe capstone. Gated on the app existing and having survived real gigs. → StageRig

What ships first, and why

Modules 1–4 as a complete standalone thing. A four-module course that exists beats an eight-module course that is perpetually nearly ready, and modules 5–8 become the upgrade path rather than the excuse for a delay.

The honest risk, stated before you ask

Two of the eight modules point at software that does not exist yet — Sections & Time and StageRig — and one points at an app whose render pass has not been verified against a real print.

No module gets recorded for an app that does not exist and work. Build the app, verify it in a real session, then record the module. Teaching a tool the student then cannot use is the fastest way to destroy the only thing this whole project has going for it.

Why me and not someone else

Because I am not a plugin developer who also makes music. I am a working producer and educator with a decade of released, credited records — Lagori, Rudy Wallang, Soul Circle Collective, an MJ tribute show — and about eighty riff and tutorial project folders behind them. I play live. The sessions in the course are the sessions the records came out of.

And the tools exist because my own sessions were slow, not because anyone surveyed a market. Everything in the audit write-up happened to me first.

Where it lives

learnmusic.nathanielschool.com. Nothing is on sale there yet, and there is no waiting list to join, no countdown and no launch price expiring at midnight. When modules 1–4's apps are all verified working, it opens.

In the meantime the tools are free, and the engineering write-up is the most useful thing here.
