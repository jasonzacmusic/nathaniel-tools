StageRig

A live keyboard rig that runs inside REAPER: one project per concert, patches as track folders, and a patch change you can make in the middle of a phrase without cutting the note off.

In development · 0.1.0 apps/StageRig.lua Requires ReaImGui Note path in JSFX
What this page is not claiming

StageRig is in development. It has not played a gig. Version 0.1.0 exists, installs, and switches patches on a bench. That is not the same thing.

It is not a MainStage replacement and will not be described as one here until a working keyboardist has played real paid shows on it and stopped carrying a backup rig. Everything before that is a demo, including this page.

The problem

I have years of concerts in MainStage and no confidence that the next macOS update won't take them with it.

The reason this is being built at all

MainStage is macOS-only, AU-only, and visibly neglected — the forums are full of “has this been abandoned” threads and update-broke-my-concert stories. Its sounds are Logic's sounds, which means the rig is not portable and never will be.

REAPER's existing answers are thin. SWS LiveConfigs has documentation frozen since 2012 and is half-broken on macOS. cfillion's Song Switcher is the only living script in the space. The largest community attempt at a full live rig stalled in 2019 on switch dropouts — which is the honest hard part, and the reason nobody has finished one.

The architecture

Locked. These are decisions, not options.

  • One project, one concertPatches are track folders inside a single project. Project tabs and subprojects were both evaluated for live switching and rejected — the switch latency and state loss are not acceptable on stage.
  • Nothing is built mid-setEverything that will ever make a sound is instantiated at load. No plugin is created, destroyed or reconfigured while the show is running.
  • The setlist is dataIt lives in project ExtState, decoupled from the timeline, so reordering the set never touches audio routing. It is parsed defensively: a corrupt setlist may lose the order, it may never lose sound.
  • Two active patches, alwaysIdle patches are never taken offline. Anticipative FX off. The budget is two simultaneously sounding patches at any moment.
  • One state machineA single deferred loop owns every transition. UI, MIDI and OSC handlers do not act — they post intents to a queue. That design removes most of the race conditions that make naive live-switching scripts unreliable.

The switch, in order

  1. Unmute B — the incoming patch — first.
  2. Gate A's input with a JSFX, so no new notes reach the outgoing patch.
  3. Let A ring for 1.5–3 seconds, or until it falls below −60 dB, then mute it.
  4. Send matching note-offs and CC64 = 0 to A.

What makes step 4 possible is a note-tracker JSFX sitting on the master key input, holding a bitmap of every note down plus the sustain state. On a switch it emits the note-offs that actually match what is held, optionally re-strikes those notes on the incoming patch, and replays the sustain pedal. That is what makes a patch change in the middle of a phrase survivable rather than a stab.

The note path is JSFX, at audio rate. It is never Lua. reaper.defer ticks at UI cadence, which is 15–30 ms of decision latency — fine for driving a stage display, not fine for anything a note passes through.

What is already built

Version 0.1.0 is a working chain from a MainStage file to a REAPER project you can play, in five pieces:

  • Concert decoderReads a MainStage .concert bundle and prints the whole rig — setlist, every patch, every channel strip, every instrument. tools/concert_report.py
  • Rig specConcert plus an editable substitution map, out to a rig description. Every mapping was probed against REAPER rather than assumed. tools/stagerig_spec.py
  • Note tracker JSFXPer-channel held-note tracking, the input gate, and the note-off + CC64 flush. This is the piece that makes a mid-phrase switch survivable. Nathaniel Tools/jsfx/nph_note_tracker.jsfx
  • BuilderBuilds the patch folders, instruments and the ExtState setlist into an empty project. It refuses to run into a project that already has tracks. NPH_StageRig Build.lua
  • Stage viewThe switch state machine and the performance display, plus soft and hard panic. apps/StageRig.lua

Eighteen checks pass against it inside REAPER. That is a bench result, not a stage result, and the two are not interchangeable.

Three things the build measured rather than assumed

  • ImGui_Col_TabActive and ImGui_ChildFlags_Border are gone in Dear ImGui 1.92.1. All three windowed apps in the suite would have crashed on first open. There is now a small bridge library that absorbs the constant renames.
  • Several plugins scan but will not instantiate. Analog Lab V, Vital, Syntronik and Axxess are all present on this Mac and none of them can be loaded by REAPER here — so there is no B3 substitute available, contrary to what the plan assumed. The organ parts are carried by Omnisphere and labelled in the rig as stand-ins.
  • Main_SaveProjectEx does not clear the dirty flag. A script that closes its own tab therefore pops a modal and blocks. The builder leaves the tab open instead.

What ports, and what does not

Run against a real, current concert with 57 patches:

Sound in the concertPatchesIn REAPER
Pianoteq 8 — every piano, EP and clav19Loads as-is. Same plugin, same presets, zero loss.
Vintage B3 (Logic)10No B3 could be instantiated on this machine. Carried by Omnisphere and labelled a stand-in.
Alchemy pads, synths, leads (Logic)16Omnisphere, or resample — the concert bundle carries its own samples folder.
Logic Sampler — strings, horns, sitar, choir15Kontakt or UVI equivalents, or resample the exact articulations.

Roughly 40% of that rig ports losslessly and the rest is substitution and honest resampling work. Every substitution lives in an explicit, editable mapping file and is reported per patch. Nothing is guessed silently, and a patch whose sound could not be mapped arrives as a clearly named empty track rather than as something that sounds nearly right.

Honest weaknesses

Stated up front, because they do not go away by not mentioning them.

  • REAPER has no sample-accurate patch-crossfade primitive. The gate-and-ring sequence above is an approximation of one. It is good; it is not the same thing.
  • Lua decision latency is 15–30 ms. Acceptable for patch switching only because the note path never goes through Lua.
  • ExtState is a text blob. The loader validates it or falls back to a flat setlist with every patch available.
  • A faithful sonic clone of Logic-only instruments is impossible. That is a licensing fact, not an engineering gap. Substitution is explicit and per-patch, and stated in the report.
  • The substitutes are worse than the plan assumed. The B3 was supposed to be covered by an organ plugin already owned. None of them load. Ten patches are currently carried by something that is honestly not the same sound.

What “done” means

Version zero is five patches from a real set, chosen because between them they exercise every substitution problem in the rig. It is finished when those five can be played back to back, switching blind, and the switching is indistinguishable from MainStage. Sounds may honestly differ where a substitute was used. The switching may not.

After that there is a stage test, and it has one gate: real paid shows, no backup rig in the car. Until that has happened, the status on this page stays exactly where it is.

Install

In REAPER: Extensions → ReaPack → Import repositories…

https://github.com/jasonzacmusic/nathaniel-tools/raw/main/index.xml

Two entries appear in the action list: Script: Nathaniel Tools StageRig Build, which builds a rig into an empty project, and Script: Nathaniel Tools StageRig, the stage view. The concert decoder is a Python tool in the repository — tools/concert_report.py — and reads your .concert files without REAPER involved at all.

Install it, look at it, tell me what breaks. Do not take it to a gig yet.
