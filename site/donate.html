If it saved you an afternoon

The suite is free, MIT-licensed, and stays that way. There is no pro tier waiting behind this page and nothing is gated. If you would like to send something anyway, this is where.

What it actually pays for

Maintenance time, which is the unglamorous part nobody funds and everybody needs.

  • Keeping the free core working across REAPER versions. An API changes, a command ID moves, an extension is updated, and something that worked for two years stops. Finding that before you do is work.
  • Running the test harness on machines that are not mine. The crash-safety guarantees only mean something if they are re-proved on a different install, a different REAPER version, a different set of extensions.
  • Answering the thread. Bug reports, install problems, the sessions people send in that break something. That is the part that makes a suite worth installing rather than a folder of scripts worth ignoring.

What it does not buy

Nothing. Deliberately. No early access, no priority support queue, no feature votes, no badge. Everything the suite does is in the free core for everyone, and a donation does not move you ahead of anyone who did not send one.

That is the SWS arrangement and it is the right one. The tools buy trust; the course is where the money is supposed to come from.

How

There is no payment link on this page yet. Setting one up properly — the right rail, the right currency, no subscription trap — has not been done, and I would rather leave the honest gap than point you at something half-configured.

If you want to send something now, or you would like to know when there is a link, write to music@nathanielschool.com and I will tell you exactly where it goes.

Three things worth more than money

  • Break it and tell meA session where something behaves wrong is worth more than a donation. Especially a large one, or an unusual folder structure, or a REAPER version I do not have.
  • Tell one personSuites live and die on word of mouth in one forum thread. If a tool here saved you time, saying so where other engineers read is the whole marketing budget.
  • Take the codeIt is MIT. The pointer-safety library and the folder arithmetic are the two pieces most worth stealing, and both are written to be readable rather than clever.
