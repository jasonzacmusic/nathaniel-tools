# Research: how Jacob Collier and other one-person multitrackers work — and REAPER grouping in depth

Compiled 19-Aug-2026 for the Nathaniel Tools teaching notes. Sources after each claim.

## 1. How Jacob Collier records and produces

**DAW and philosophy.** Collier has used Logic since he was 11 and calls it more central to his creative life than any instrument: "I'd almost say that that workspace is more prevalent in my creative life than any musical instrument" (https://www.mixonline.com/recording/jacob-collier-making-elements-co-exist). He prefers Logic partly because of custom key commands: "you can make your own keyboard shortcuts, and that's why I'm so anti-Pro Tools" (https://www.soundonsound.com/people/jacob-collier). He mixes in the box, mostly stock Logic plus a few staples (Altiverb "Notre Dame", Soundtoys, Waves, iZotope RX, Xfer LFO Tool, XLN RC-20) (https://www.soundonsound.com/people/jacob-collier; https://dropcue.app/composers/jacob-collier).

**Scale.** 646 tracks for "All I Need" (https://x.com/jacobcollier/status/1263209264467701760), 316 tracks / 2,781 regions for the a cappella "Bridge Over Troubled Water" — "quite chill by my standards" (https://www.musicradar.com/news/jacob-collier-bridge-over-troubled-water-logic-pro-session), 150 simultaneous vocal tracks in "Chestnuts Roasting" (https://x.com/jacobcollier/status/1338219954877980673), "between about 300 and 700" tracks per song on Djesse Vol. 3 (https://blog.dropbox.com/topics/our-community/jacob-collier-remote-collaboration). He hit Logic's old 253/256 audio-track ceiling constantly ("Almost every song on the album is over 256 tracks and I have to bounce things down") (https://www.soundonsound.com/people/jacob-collier; https://x.com/jacobcollier/status/924317877590745088). Workarounds: separate Logic projects per song section (https://www.mixonline.com/recording/jacob-collier-making-elements-co-exist), and bouncing sub-sessions down – e.g. a 293-track percussion session for "All Night Long" bounced to four tracks in the main session (https://grahamstoney.com/music/how-a-real-musician-uses-the-logic-pro-digital-audio-workstation).

**Organisation habits.** He is candid that his sessions are "part extreme organization, and part complete chaos" – hundreds of tracks, thousands of regions, half the tracks unlabelled (https://tapeop.com/interviews/151/jacob-collier). What IS systematic: in "Moon River" the audio samples were "arranged by frequency from low to high, grouped into folders for each of the diatonic notes in the key", and vocal parts were "grouped into busses for common effect processing" such as EQ and octave-shifting (https://grahamstoney.com/music/how-a-real-musician-uses-the-logic-pro-digital-audio-workstation). Notes are stacked vertically adjacent in the arrange window and panned L/C/R at varying degrees (same source). Collaboration is stem-based via Dropbox: "I'll send them stems... They will send back a bunch of stems... Then I can drop into the session" (https://blog.dropbox.com/topics/our-community/jacob-collier-remote-collaboration). *Uncertain:* no text source confirms whether he uses Track Stacks (summing vs folder) or a fixed colour scheme; the session-breakdown videos (https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PLHX_dBxnc8z8A5TyQUNy7_rqkOSY_F6wF) are where to verify.

**Recording process.** Harmony often written in advance; he records the lead, then layers intervals one voice at a time (fifths, thirds, sevenths, extensions, microtonal layers, doubles) (https://dropcue.app/composers/jacob-collier). Some voicings planned on a MIDI keyboard first; others improvised (https://grahamstoney.com/...). "Moon River" was tracked without a click "to make the work flow freely" (same). Simple signal chain: one mic, no input compression – "200 tracks, multi instruments, on one SM58 and it sounds good" (https://www.soundonsound.com/people/jacob-collier). He records acoustic sources and leaves them largely alone: "I don't tend to mess around with them. I don't add a ton of plug-ins" (same). Tuning is manual, in cents, using Logic's Time and Pitch Machine rather than Flex Pitch or Auto-Tune ("13 cents sharp, I'll... minus 13, enter"), and he refuses timing quantisation – "some of the most gorgeous grooves are wonky" (same). Mixing leans on "automation in post... I don't tend to touch the desks", with heavy volume automation on every track (https://www.soundonsound.com/people/jacob-collier; https://grahamstoney.com/...). Software instruments serve as placeholders later replaced by live orchestra (score exported to Finale) (https://grahamstoney.com/...).

## 2. How other multitrackers organise Logic and REAPER sessions

**Andrew Scheps (REAPER, Puremix template).** Colour-coded folders per family (dark blue drums, dark green electric gtr, light green acoustic), pre-configured track groups and VCA masters for Drums, Bass, Guitars, Lead Vox, BVs, Keys, Perc; each family has aux/parallel tracks; everything except drums/sub-bass also feeds a parallel "Rear Bus" (https://www.puremix.com/blog/andrew-scheps-mix-template-2025-the-art-of-letting-go; https://forum.cockos.com/showthread.php?t=193345). Template starts with all processing off: "Templates don't make mixes, decisions do."

**Sonarworks/REAPER tracking template.** Folders as buses per instrument; consistent colours (drums red, bass blue, ac gtr lime, el gtr yellow, keys green, strings cyan, lead vox purple, BVs pink, FX orange); folder track set as **Record Arm Lead** with children as **Record Arm Follow** (Shift+G) so one click arms the whole kit; section markers (Shift+M) used as punch-in points with pre-roll; vocal takes on separate tracks in a VOX folder (https://www.sonarworks.com/blog/learn/optimize-reaper-for-recording-with-templates-plus-a-session-changing-tip).

**General pro convention (Sonarworks).** Left-to-right order drums→bass→guitars→keys→vocals→FX→buses; separate tracking/editing/mixing templates; `BUS_DRUMS`, `BUS_VOCALS` naming; markers Intro/Verse/Chorus; stems printed as individual tracks + buses + full mix (https://www.sonarworks.com/blog/learn/daw-session-organization-pro-workflow-asset-management).

**Kenny Beats** – Ableton templates (main + vocal-production template) as the core speed tool (https://splice.com/blog/kenny-beats-workflow-secrets-templates/). **Finneas** – Logic Pro X in a bedroom; harmonies mixed level with the lead, doubles panned hard L/R (https://www.mixonline.com/recording/finneas-on-producing-billie-eilishs-number-one-album-in-his-bedroom). **Charlie Puth** – finished records are Pro Tools; he stacks vocal layers, aligns by eye, then adds a "natural" take so it doesn't sound robotic (https://www.musicradar.com/news/charlie-puth-vocals-quincy-jones-michael-jackson). **Jack Conte/Pomplamoose** – assembly-line: "an hour to arrange, an hour to record" per song (https://brainpick.castos.com/episodes/episode-014). **Andrew Huang** – preset "slots" so he's one click from vocal-tracking or synth-tracking setups (https://www.proaudiospotlight.com/inside-andrew-huangs-content-creation-studio/). **Kenny Gioia** – official REAPER videos "Folders vs. Busses vs. Groups vs. VCAs", "Multi-Track Drum Recording and Comping in Lanes", "Comping Double & Triple Vocals", "Project Templates", "Track Templates" (https://www.reaper.fm/videos.php). **Jon Tidey** – ReaperBlog track-template library and lanes workflow (https://reaper.blog/2023/10/record-track-lanes/).

## 3. REAPER grouping in depth (REAPER 7)

**(a) Folders (parent/child).** Audio actually flows: children sum into the parent, so the parent is a bus you can EQ/compress and whose fader moves everything. Create: click the folder icon on the track panel, drag tracks onto a parent, or right-click → **Move tracks to folder → New folder track** (new in v7). Collapse with the small arrow above the track number; **Preferences → Appearance → Track Control Panel** sets the cycle. Nesting is unlimited. Caveat: folder fader/automation doesn't scale post-fader sends, so reverb balance can drift (https://reaper.blog/2016/06/audio-routing-explained/; https://www.soundonsound.com/techniques/reaper-vca-grouping-linking; https://www.reaper.fm/userguide.php).

**(b) Track grouping.** Select tracks → **Shift+G** (Track grouping parameters) or **View → Track Grouping Matrix (Cmd+Alt+G)**; up to 128 groups. Parameters: Volume, Pan, Width, Mute, Solo, Record Arm, Polarity, Automation Mode, Media/Razor Edits, each as Lead/Follow, with Reverse options. Shift-drag overrides a group temporarily; **Track → Track grouping enabled** disables all globally; **View → Track Group Manager** names/colours groups and shows/hides each group's tracks in TCP or Mixer. Limitation: automation on the lead doesn't drive followers (https://www.reapertips.com/post/reaper-v6-72-tons-of-new-track-group-features; SOS link above).

**(c) VCA.** Same matrix, VCA Lead/Follow columns (+ "VCA pre-FX follow"). No audio passes through the VCA track; followers' faders don't move but their gain is added; envelopes on the VCA lead apply to all followers — the clean way to ride a whole choir or horn section (User Guide 5.16; SOS link).

**(d) Selection linking.** Selecting several tracks makes a temporary Lead/Follow group (User Guide 5.15). Folder-aware selection: SWS "Select children of selected folder track(s)"; Track Group Manager options "Select tracks in group when selecting group" and "Add/remove child tracks when adding/removing parent folder". Grouping toolbar (Alt+Shift+G): "Automatically group selected tracks for media/razor editing"; 7.74 added "Automatically group folder/child tracks" (https://reaper.blog/2026/06/774-update/).

**(e) Buses via sends.** A plain track fed by sends (Routing Matrix, Alt+R) – free positioning, parallel processing, tracks can feed several buses (https://www.soundonsound.com/techniques/groups-folders-matrices).

**For a musician layering piano/vocals/horns/strings/choir:** folders per family (SWS Auto Color rule "(children) → parent" so children inherit colour, https://www.reapertips.com/post/how-to-auto-color-in-reaper), Record-Arm grouping on the folder for one-click arming, VCA leads for balance/automation rides across families, and sends for shared reverbs.

**REAPER 7 features.** *Fixed item lanes*: right-click TCP → **Fixed item lanes**; set **Options → New recording that overlaps existing media items → Add lanes**; up to 128 lanes; lane menu → **Comping → Comp into new empty lane**, then swipe; comps are new items, originals untouched (https://www.reapertips.com/post/reaper-7; https://reaper.blog/2023/10/record-track-lanes/). *Razor edits*, *Track spacers*, *FX containers*, and Track Manager also arrived in 7 (https://www.reapertips.com/post/reaper-7).

**Recommended scripts.** SWS: Auto Color/Icon/Layout, "Make folder from selected tracks", "Select children of selected folder track(s)", save/restore track selection (https://sws-extension.org/color.php). Via ReaPack: X-Raym, MPL, cfillion (https://reapack.com/).
