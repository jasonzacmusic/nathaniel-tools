01What each one does, in a sentence

Teal · look

Palette & Look

Colours and names your tracks from what they are — bass, bus, reverb return, harmony — with your own rules on top. LIVE mode keeps doing it as you add tracks.

Use it when a session grew past twenty tracks and you can't find the guitars at a glance.

Green · structure

Folders & Flow

Folders without dragging: tick → make folder, indent/outdent, move a whole folder, isolate one, dissolve, and repair a session where a folder swallowed everything after it.

Use it when you're shaping a session — drum kit, harmony stack, horns.

Green · structure

Track Settings Transfer

Pro Tools' "Import Session Data": copy FX chains, volume, pan and sends from one project tab to another, matched by track name; builds missing tracks. Guesses stay unticked, transfer asks first.

Use it when last week's session has the vocal chain this week's should have.

Amber · delivery

Stem Print & Handoff

Prints stems (raw / inserts / fully wet / front-end) at unity fader and centre pan, then puts every fader, pan, mute and bypass back. Builds a plugin-free handoff tab with the files on it.

Use it when something leaves your room — mixer, collaborator, another DAW.

Amber · delivery

MIDI Batch Export

Many .mid files in one go, named by pattern, with tempo and time signature from where each item sits. Shows every filename before writing; won't overwrite by accident.

Use it when you want every riff or idea as its own MIDI file.

Violet · performance

StageRig (early)

Live patch switching that lets the outgoing patch ring out instead of chopping it. NOW / NEXT in big letters, GO / PANIC / ALL OFF, Space = GO, footswitch scripts. Your NSM Tribute Night setlist builds in 10 seconds with real instruments.

Honest: works here; hasn't played a paid show yet.

The speed layer (your keys): A Solo Focus · ⇧A/pedal Record Arm Toggle · NumPad 6 FX Float · ⇧F/pedal FX Open/Close All · N/B Region Next/Prev · M/pedal Marker at Bar · ⌘⇧T Tempo at Bar · ⇧⌃M MIDI Render · ⌘⌃V Flush Paste · ⇧D Duplicate Track (empty + armed) · E Unsolo & Unselect · pedal Toggle Chorale. 04How it fits your way of working

You record real instruments in layers — one player, many passes, hundreds of tracks — the same shape as Jacob Collier's sessions. The order that works, with the tools that help each step:

  1. Start from a template, never empty. Families as folders (DRUMS, BASS, KEYS, VOCALS, HARMONY, HORNS, STRINGS, FX RETURNS), colours already set, a reverb and a delay bus already wired. Collier bounces sub-sessions down and Andrew Scheps starts every mix from a template with all processing off — "templates don't make mixes, decisions do". Save one as a Project Template today; the next app below builds it for you.
  2. Record layers fast. ⇧D Duplicate Track gives you the next layer empty and armed; ⇧A arms only what you're on; A solos it in place; E resets everything. Collier tracks one voice at a time on one SM58 and does not touch plugins while tracking — the point is speed of the next take, not the sound yet.
  3. Comp in lanes, not takes. REAPER 7's fixed item lanes: right-click the track → Fixed item lanes, and set Options → New recording that overlaps existing media items → Add lanes. Then lane menu → Comping → Comp into new empty lane and swipe the best bits. Originals stay untouched.
  4. Name and colour as you go — Palette & Look with LIVE on. Half of Collier's tracks are famously unnamed; his fix was folders and buses per family and "arranged by frequency from low to high". Yours can be automatic.
  5. Shape it — Folders & Flow: harmony stack into a folder, horns into a folder; the folder track is the bus (children sum into it), so a compressor on HARMONY glues the stack.
  6. Group what should move together — see the lesson in §06: folder for sound, group for behaviour, VCA for rides.
  7. Hand it off — Stem Print & Handoff (Inserts only, unity fader, handoff tab). Collier collaborates by stems through Dropbox: "I'll send them stems… they send back stems… I drop them into the session."

05How Jacob Collier and the others actually do it (research, sources in the repo)

Jacob Collier — Logic since age 11; the DAW "more prevalent in my creative life than any musical instrument". 316 tracks / 2,781 regions for a cappella Bridge Over Troubled Water ("quite chill by my standards"), 646 tracks for All I Need, 300–700 per song on Djesse Vol. 3. He hits Logic's track ceiling constantly and bounces sub-sessions down (a 293-track percussion session became four tracks). Sessions are "part extreme organization, part complete chaos": vocal parts grouped into buses for shared EQ/octave processing, samples in folders per note of the key, notes stacked adjacent and panned L/C/R by degree. Records one voice at a time, harmony often written first, "200 tracks on one SM58". Tunes by hand in cents, refuses to quantise time ("some of the most gorgeous grooves are wonky"), mixes with volume automation on every track and hardly touches faders. Custom key commands are the reason he is "so anti-Pro Tools".
Andrew Scheps (REAPER) — colour-coded folders per family, pre-built track groups and VCA masters for Drums / Bass / Guitars / Lead Vox / BVs / Keys / Perc, every family with its own parallel bus, everything (except drums and sub) also feeding a "rear bus". Template starts with all processing off. Tracking template convention — folder as bus per instrument, folder set as Record Arm lead with children as followers so one click arms the kit; section markers as punch points; vocal takes on separate tracks in a VOX folder; left-to-right order drums → bass → guitars → keys → vocals → FX → buses; separate tracking / editing / mixing templates. Finneas: harmonies level with the lead, doubles hard L/R. Charlie Puth: stacks aligned by eye then one "natural" take so it doesn't sound robotic. Kenny Beats / Andrew Huang: templates and one-click preset slots as the core speed tool.

Full notes with every source: docs/research/research_multitrack_workflows.md in the repository.

06The grouping lesson — which kind does what

REAPER has four different things people call "grouping". They are not interchangeable. Rule of thumb for a musician layering piano, vocals, horns, strings, choir:

KindWhat it really isUse it forHow (REAPER 7)
FolderAudio flows: the children sum into the parent. The parent is a bus with a fader and its own FX. Collapse it to tidy the screen.Sound. HARMONY folder = one compressor and one reverb send for the whole stack; DRUMS folder = the kit as one fader.Click the folder button on a track's panel, drag tracks onto it, or right-click → Move tracks to folder → New folder track. Folders & Flow does all of it by ticking. Caveat: the folder fader does not scale post-fader sends, so reverb balance can drift when you pull the folder down.
Track groupControls move together: volume, pan, mute, solo, arm, polarity, automation mode, media/razor edits — each as lead/follow. Up to 128 groups.Behaviour. Arm the whole choir with one click (folder as arm-lead, children arm-follow). Mute all four horn tracks at once. Edit the six drum tracks as one.Select tracks → ⇧G (grouping parameters) or View → Track Grouping Matrix (⌘⌥G). View → Track Group Manager names/colours groups and shows/hides them in TCP or Mixer. Hold ⇧ while dragging to override a group once; Track → Track grouping enabled switches all off. Limitation: automation on the lead does not drive the followers.
VCAA control-only master: no audio passes through it, the followers' faders don't move but their gain does; automation on the VCA applies to all followers.Rides. Riding the whole horn section against the vocal in the mix, or the entire choir, without touching each track's automation.Same matrix: the VCA lead / VCA follow columns. Make one empty track "VCA HORNS", set it VCA lead, set the horns VCA follow. Tracks added later obey the ride.
Selection linkingSelecting several tracks makes a temporary group for as long as they're selected.Quick one-off moves.Just ⌘-click / ⇧-click tracks. SWS: "Select children of selected folder track(s)". Track Group Manager option "Select tracks in group when selecting group". The grouping toolbar (⌥⇧G) has "automatically group selected tracks for media/razor editing", and 7.74 added "automatically group folder/child tracks".
Bus by sendsA plain track fed by sends from many tracks; tracks can feed several buses; free positioning.Shared effects — one reverb, one delay, one parallel compressor.Routing matrix ⌥R, or the track's I/O button → add send.
So, for your sessions: folder per family (sound and tidiness) · a track group per family with Record Arm and Mute lead/follow so the pedal or one click arms the whole kit or choir · a VCA per family for mix rides · one reverb bus and one delay bus by sends. Colour by family (Palette & Look) so the groups and the folders read the same at a glance. Then save the empty version as a project template.

Answers to the doubts people usually have here

  • "If I put tracks in a folder, do I still need a group?" Only if you want the controls to move together. A folder moves the sound as one; a group moves the buttons as one. Most families want both.
  • "Why does my reverb get louder when I pull the folder down?" Post-fader sends inside the folder are not scaled by the folder fader. Use a VCA for the ride instead, or send from the folder track.
  • "Can I automate a group?" Not through the group — automate the VCA lead. That is the whole reason VCAs exist.
  • "How do I stop a group from firing while I fix one track?" Hold ⇧ on the control for a one-off, or Track → Track grouping enabled to switch every group off temporarily.
  • "Takes or lanes?" Lanes (REAPER 7). Comping in lanes never touches the originals; convert an old take-based track with Track properties: Fixed item lanes (convert takes to lanes).

07What we could build next — five ideas that fit the way you record

1 · Session Template Builder

One click: your family folders, colours, record-arm groups, VCAs, reverb + delay buses, all named and coloured — from a short list you edit ("VOCALS 8, HARMONY 12, HORNS 4, STRINGS 6"). Saves as a Project Template. Ends the "start from empty" habit the research says every fast multitracker has already killed.

2 · Layer Recorder

Collier-style tracking pedal: one key/pedal = "next layer": duplicates the current track empty, keeps the input and monitoring, arms it, names it Harmony 07, drops it in the right folder, moves the cursor back to the section start, and rolls with pre-roll. Built on Duplicate Track + Record Arm Toggle, which already exist.

3 · Harmony Ladder

Shows the stack you've sung so far as a chord: which notes are covered, which are missing (the 3rd? the 9th?), per section. Reads the MIDI you sing against or the pitch of the audio. Makes 20-voice stacks plannable instead of memorised.

4 · Session Health

The tests we already run for the apps, turned on your session: unnamed tracks, tracks with no colour, folders that never close, sends to nowhere, armed-but-muted tracks, files not in the project folder, plugins that failed to load, sample-rate mismatches. One page, one click, before you record.

5 · Group Deck

A window that shows your family groups as big buttons: arm / mute / solo / VCA per family, and "isolate this family" — the mixer for a one-person band. Grouping matrix is powerful but nobody enjoys it live.
